# Synesthesia Project: Current Working Minimum Viable Instrument

*Original brief by Freeman (dancer, artist, somatic educator), September 2026.
Reproduced verbatim for the build team; only formatting was adjusted.*

## Purpose

The Synesthesia Project (SP) is an experimental audiovisual instrument for exploring movement as perceptual material. It begins with the proposition that the kinetic signature of any movement can be applied to responsive fields of color, shape, and sound. The moving source itself disappears; its audiovisual wake remains.

The first MVP is deliberately limited. It is a laptop-based instrument intended for artistic research and repeated independent use at home. Whether its initial movement input is recorded or live will be determined with the development collaborator.

The kinetic signature of the movement remains the experimental constant.

## Core inquiry

What becomes perceptible when the same movement is expressed through different audiovisual materials, while the visible source of that movement is absent?

SP does not diagnose, classify, or assign meaning to a participant's state. It is an artistic and perceptual instrument. The participant remains the authority on what, if anything, becomes newly noticeable.

## Working distinction

SP does not claim to reproduce neurological synaesthesia or to speak for the experiences of synaesthetes. Its working title refers to cross-sensory translation: movement expressed through visual and auditory forms.

## 1. Movement becomes a kinetic signature

Movement enters the instrument through an initial input method to be determined during prototyping: either a short recorded clip or a live camera feed. The movement source may be human or non-human: a wink, a hand moving through water, a curtain, a shadow, or drops entering liquid.

The instrument extracts movement information such as timing, direction, velocity, acceleration, scale, continuity, density, expansion, contraction, and spatial relationship. This organized movement information is the kinetic signature.

The signature is not a verbal description such as graceful, anxious, or spiral. It is movement-derived information that can remain stable while other conditions change.

**Physical analogy.** Imagine moving a finger through a bowl of water. SP captures the kinetic signature of that movement, not the finger and not the water. Now imagine applying that same signature to a bowl of honey. The movement remains constant, but the material responds differently. SP extends this principle into fields of color, shape, and sound.

## 2. Signature is applied to responsive material

The kinetic signature acts as a set of forces upon two parallel responsive fields: visual material and auditory material. Neither is merely an illustration or accompaniment to the other. Together they form the audiovisual wake.

Visual material is a responsive collection of attributes that may take form as smoke, bubbles, ribbons, particles, filaments, flocking forms, color, or light. Adjustable visual properties may include viscosity, rigidity, elasticity, density, dispersion, persistence, decay, expansion, compression, brightness, and spatial spread.

Auditory material is a responsive collection of sonic attributes that may take form as tone, texture, pulse, resonance, breath, noise, harmony, or spatialized sound. Adjustable auditory properties may include pitch range, timbre, intensity, density, rhythm, sustain, decay, elasticity, compression, and spatial behavior.

The same kinetic information may act differently upon each field while preserving the integrity of the originating signature.

**Signature is not material.** The same kinetic signature applied to different materials produces different wakes. This allows direct comparison without changing the originating movement.

## 3. Artist workflow

1. **Introduce.** Bring movement into the instrument through the selected initial input method: recorded or live.
2. **Prepare.** Establish a usable movement sample; if recorded, trim it and adjust playback speed where useful.
3. **Extract.** Derive and preserve the kinetic signature independently of the visible source video.
4. **Select.** Choose a visual material and an auditory material from a small initial library.
5. **Apply.** Use the kinetic signature to activate the selected materials.
6. **Play.** Adjust material properties while repeatedly watching and listening to the response.
7. **Compare.** Re-render the same signature through other materials or parameter settings.
8. **Preserve.** Save settings, experiments, and finished audiovisual compositions; export as MP4.

The artist observes the audiovisual response, adjusts material properties, repeats the same kinetic signature, and compares the resulting compositions. The input method should support this iterative way of learning to play the instrument.

## 4. Minimum MVP scope

- One practical movement-input path (recorded clip or live camera feed) selected with the development collaborator for this iteration.
- Laptop operation using Freeman's existing MacBook Pro where practical.
- A small library of approximately 3–5 visual materials and 1–5 auditory materials.
- A simple artist-facing interface with meaningful adjustable properties.
- Preservation of the kinetic signature independently from any one rendering.
- Replay and re-render of the same signature through different materials.
- Save/load of movement, settings, and experiments; MP4 export.
- Training and documentation sufficient for independent use.
- An architecture that can later accept additional materials and movement sources.

## 5. First artistic investigation

The first body of work will use one captured kinetic signature (perhaps a smile or a wink) to create an album of approximately 20–30 audiovisual compositions. Each composition will begin with the same signature and a defined baseline. Chance procedures may determine the selected visual material, auditory material, and the properties available for modulation.

*Use what we build to create an album of 20–30 compositions all derived from the same kinetic signature.*

The album is both artwork and research record. It will retain discoveries, failed mappings, rough places, revisions, and the gradual process of learning to play the instrument.

## 6. Questions the prototype should help answer

- How does the kinetic signature remain perceptibly consistent as it acts upon substantially different visual and auditory materials?
- Which properties allow a material to reveal rather than obscure the kinetic signature?
- What auditory behaviors meaningfully correspond to visual qualities such as viscosity, rigidity, elasticity, expansion, and compression?
- What becomes perceptible when the moving source is removed but its kinetic effects remain active?
- How much control is necessary for expressive use without making the interface technically burdensome?
- What does the artist learn by holding movement constant and changing the sensory conditions through which it is encountered?

## 7. Explicitly beyond this first MVP

The first MVP does not need to solve live full-body tracking, multi-camera depth, public-participant operation, a gallery-ready installation, multi-user interaction, polished branding, a finished visual language, clinical interpretation, diagnosis, or treatment.

These may belong to later stages. The immediate goal is smaller: a reliable, playable instrument that makes the central artistic proposition testable.

## 8. Relevant precedents and technical references

These examples illustrate separate artistic or technical problems that others have addressed. None constitutes SP, and none should determine its aesthetic or architecture. They are offered so a development team can learn from existing approaches rather than reinvent every layer.

- **Sonic Moves.** Demonstrates accessible movement-derived audiovisual interaction. SP differs by removing the source, preserving a reusable recorded signature, and separating signature from adjustable material.
- **Forms (2011), Quayola and Memo Akten.** Shows recorded athletic movement transformed into abstract forms extending beyond the body. It is an artistic precedent for motion traces, not a model for a playable user instrument.
- **TouchDesigner: optical flow controlling particles.** Demonstrates motion information from video driving visual behavior. For SP, the extracted signature must remain separable from the chosen visual material.
- **TouchDesigner: fluid simulation in TOPs.** Demonstrates a responsive simulated material with adjustable behavior. Fluid is one example material, not SP's prescribed aesthetic.
- **IRCAM MuBu.** Demonstrates multimodal sound and motion analysis, synthesis, and gesture-based sonic systems. It is a technical reference, not a decision that SP must use Max.

**Development principle.** Before choosing an architecture, examine what can be learned, adapted, or reused from these examples while preserving SP's central requirement: one reusable kinetic signature remains constant as different visual and auditory materials, and their properties, are changed.
