# Walkthrough video script (~15 minutes)

For Braden to record for Freeman: a screen recording of Chrome with your voice over it.
Record at the window size Freeman uses. Before recording: have a real wink clip ready (your
phone, "Most Compatible"), start from a fresh library (or a Chrome profile with none of
your test work), and turn notifications off.

Speak slowly; pause after each action so the picture settles. Times are a guide.

---

## 1. Welcome (0:00 – 1:00)

*Open the instrument. Let the dedication fade.*

> "This is Synesthesia, version zero, made for you. It's built on your write-up: a
> movement becomes a signature, and that one signature moves through different materials,
> water or honey, and through sound. The clip disappears; its wake remains. It's a first
> playable version for you to co-direct. Everything I decided while building it is written
> down in a decisions file, so you can change your mind about any of it."

*Show the introduction's three steps, then Skip.*

## 2. The Library and a clip (1:00 – 3:30)

> "This is the Library, home base. Let's bring in a clip."

*Drag your wink clip onto the window.*

> "Trim to where the movement happens. The preview loops inside the trim."

*Drag the trim handles tightly around the wink.*

> "For a wink, box the eye, so only its movement is used."

*Draw a box around the eye.*

> "Speed only changes how fast it plays by default, not what's captured."

*Click Extract signature. While it runs:*

> "It's reading the movement frame by frame. It keeps no picture of the clip, only
> where things moved, which way, how fast, and when."

## 3. The bare wake (3:30 – 5:00)

> "This is the bare wake: a stroke for each part of the frame, pointing the way it moved.
> Here's the close… and the open. Below are the movement's lines: energy, direction,
> expanding and contracting, how smooth it is, and how much of the frame moves. These marks
> are the moments of sudden movement."

*Toggle Hide source off and on.*

> "Hide source shows the clip beside it, if you want to compare. Let's name it and open it
> in the Studio."

*Name it "Wink 01", click Save and open in Studio.*

## 4. The Studio (5:00 – 9:00)

*Wait for "Getting to know this computer…" to finish. Press Space.*

> "Here's the wink moving through water, and its sound. Same movement, now as a wake."

*Let it loop twice.*

> "On the right: the visual material and the sound material. Let's try honey."

*Switch Visual to Honey, then Sound to Honey.*

> "Same movement, a different substance. It sags and holds; the sound stutters and drags.
> Your 'Breee-weet' and 'Broo-roo-roo'."

*Move Viscosity slowly from low to high while it plays.*

> "With Linked on, one slider moves sight and sound together, because each property means
> the same thing in both. Viscosity thickens the honey and slows the sound's glides."

*Turn Linked off, move a slider in only one field, turn it back on.*

> "Double-click any slider to return it to the baseline, where every composition starts."

*Double-click Viscosity.*

> "Snapshots let you compare. I'll store this as A…"

*Click A. Switch Visual to Smoke; click B. Then click A, B, A while it plays.*

> "…and switch between them while it plays. It never restarts."

*Open Movement: show Signature strength and Tail.*

> "Movement settings belong to the signature: how hard it pushes, how long the wake
> settles afterwards."

## 5. Chance (9:00 – 10:30)

*Click Draw by chance, then Draw.*

> "Chance can choose the materials and open a few properties for play. The rest stay at
> their baseline, locked. You can unlock one, and the composition notes that you did, so
> the record stays honest. The seed fixes the draw, so it's repeatable."

*Unlock a locked property; show the lock disappear.*

## 6. Saving, notes, presenting (10:30 – 11:30)

*Type a note: "The close reads clearly in honey." Click Save.*

> "Notes are part of the research record. Save keeps it in the Library with a picture of
> its wake."

*Press F.*

> "Present shows only the wake, full screen. Escape comes back."

*Press Esc.*

## 7. Rendering a video (11:30 – 12:30)

*Click Render MP4. Choose 1080p, a folder you made in Movies. Render.*

> "Render makes a video frame by frame, at full quality however long it takes. Even out
> loudness makes every video equally loud; turn it off to keep quiet pieces quiet."

*When it finishes, open the MP4 in QuickTime briefly.*

## 8. An album (12:30 – 14:00)

*Library › Wink 01 › New album. Keep 25 tracks and "Every pairing once". Generate.*

> "An album gathers many compositions from one signature: your first investigation.
> Chance chooses each track's materials and open properties. The same master seed always
> makes the same album."

*Open track 01 in the Studio, play, go back. Mark it Kept; mark another Set aside with a
note.*

> "Mark what you keep and what you set aside. Nothing is thrown away: set-aside tracks stay
> in the record. Batch render makes the videos unattended, and Export album log writes the
> whole record as a readable document."

## 9. Keeping it safe, and help (14:00 – 15:00)

*Library › Back up everything.*

> "Your work lives in Chrome on this computer. Back up now and then, and keep the file
> somewhere safe. Restore brings it all back."

*Show Help, then Diagnostics › Copy report.*

> "Help explains every material and property. If something goes wrong, Diagnostics has a
> report you can copy and send me. And the guide in the folder walks through all of this."

> "That's it. It's yours to play, and to change."
